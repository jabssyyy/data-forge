## The three directly relevant recent primary papers

1. Kosowski et al. (2025), [The Dragon Hatchling: The Missing Link between the Transformer and Models of the Brain](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.26507v1), Definition 4 and §6.4/Figure 14. Defines the nonnegative gated product and tests sparse activity on the synthetic task. Local measurements are not paper-scale measurements.
2. You et al. (2025), [Spark Transformer: Reactivating Sparsity in FFN and Attention](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.06644v2), abstract. Uses explicit top-k sparsity in FFN and attention. This supports a particular design comparison, not a universal statement about Transformers.
3. Mirzadeh et al. (2023), [ReLU Strikes Back: Exploiting Activation Sparsity in Large Language Models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.04564v1), abstract, submitted October 6, 2023. Directly investigates ReLU activation sparsity and ways to exploit it in inference. Its efficiency findings do not establish speedups for this browser artifact.

Full author records, exact locations and claim boundaries are in `sources.md`. Primary-source pages were opened and verified in this session; no generated or secondary summary is being counted as a primary paper.
