STRUCTURAL COMPARISON

Published equations, not a measurement. A ReLU hidden unit has one positive condition; the BDH product requires both factors to be positive.

RELU TRANSFORMER · HIDDEN ACTIVATION

h = ReLU(zW₁)

One rectified projection. This is a simplified, bias-free hidden expression; the published FFN also includes biases and an output projection.

BDH · MEASURED GATED PRODUCT

y = (Dy · LN(a*))⁺ ⊙ x

Two non-negative factors. If either is zero, the product is zero. We count this product, xy_sparse, across 1,024 neurons.

The Dragon Hatchling · Definition 4 ↗

The gate explains how zeros are possible. It does not establish why repetition changes activity, guarantee a confidence-driven collapse, or predict a Transformer’s measured ratio.

03 / THE BOUNDARIESREAD THE COUNTEREXAMPLES

What this does not show

7 disclosed limitations
  1. Layer 0 shows no effect — confirming the paper’s own statement about higher layers. Switch the layer control to 0: its phase means show no comparable drop. The shipped run measured 0.92×; we report that as no effect, not as an inversion.
  2. 64× neuron shrink (65,536 → 1,024). This small independent model measures 15.76% in memorize and 5.08% in repeat at layer 2 on the canonical sequence. Absolute rates are specific to this checkpoint and protocol.
  3. Synthetic task only — no claim is made about BDH on natural language. The corpus is a 13-letter warm-up plus a random 8-letter word, by design.
  4. BDH token 0 always reads 0.0% — attention is tril(diagonal=-1), so token 0 attends to nothing. A code property, not a finding. It is excluded from the warm-up mean on this page: the canonical warm-up mean is 19.65%. The historical 18.14% includes it. The Transformer attends to itself and can have nonzero activity at token 0.
  5. Independent reimplementation — faithful to pathwaycom/bdh with a measurement hook added and the config shrunk. Not an official BDH model.
  6. One small checkpoint and one synthetic protocol — this experiment does not establish a general law of intelligence, a causal confidence meter, or a result about later BDH-family systems.
  7. Warm-up shows the highest activity, and we cannot say why. On the canonical run, layer 2 averages 19.65% through warm-up (excluding token 0; historically 18.14% including it) against 5.08% on repeats — yet the model’s cross-entropy on the warm-up is 0.113 bits, lower than the 0.766 bits it spends on repeats. These phase means do not support a simple uncertainty explanation. Herrmann et al. also caution that next-token loss alone is a poor proxy for in-context computation complexity. An earlier draft of this note explained the gap as “activity tracks the model’s uncertainty, not the oracle’s.” We measured that and it is false. It is corrected here rather than quietly dropped. One possible explanation is state re-establishment at the start of a cold sequence — untested, and labelled as such. The claim in the bar above is scoped to the memorize → repeat transition precisely because of this gap.

What is live, and what is not

LIVE
BDH and ReLU Transformer forward passes, running in your browser. Input or weight changes compute the model; layer changes reuse its measured output. Token playback animates that output.
ANALYTIC
Oracle surprisal, in closed form from the sequence definition. No model involved.
PRECOMPUTED
Fixed trained and untrained weights shipped as JSON; offline comparison results are labelled separately.
SYNTHETIC
The letter corpus — the paper’s Section 6.4 protocol, not natural language.
